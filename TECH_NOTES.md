## 3. Two languages

Engines return codes (reasons, risks, notes, fact labels) that the interface renders through `js/i18n/en.json` and `js/i18n/de.json`. ## 5. Law snapshot

**Source route.** Because gesetze-im-internet.de does not answer the runners, `tools/snapshot-laws.mjs --archive <dir>` reads a checkout of the public daily archive QuantLaw/gesetze-im-internet (branch `data`), whose scraper stores the host's table of contents (gii-toc.xml, as data/toc.xml in the archive) and every norm's unzipped archive (under data/items/ and the norm's slug), unchanged. The bundled corpus comes from commit `0d22c0e8e411a1d43d9e586ff87ce53cc86e66f1`, scraped 2026-11-19T04:01:18Z (the author date of the commit; the scraper stamps UTC). All 31 slugs and titles of the slice match the archived table of contents; the scrape's list of files it could not download (not_found.txt) is empty. Building twice from the commit gives byte-identical files. A direct run (`node tools/snapshot-laws.mjs`) downloads from the host and checks every paragraph link with a HEAD request; `.github/workflows/snapshot.yml` runs either route and pushes to a review branch.

**Links.** The archive route cannot check links. Spot checks through the web-fetch tool on 19 November: <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/milog/__17.html>, <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/verpackdg/__20.html> and <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34c.html> show the expected paragraph; <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aentg_2009/__2a.html> returned a server error twice, so it is unconfirmed.

**DTD element mapping** (gii-norm.dtd 1.01): `dokumente` (root, `builddate`) → norm; the first `norm`'s `metadaten` → `jurabk`/`amtabk` (abbreviation, `amtabk` preferred), `langue` (title, else `kurzue`); every further `norm` with an `enbez` of § or Art. → a paragraph: `enbez`, `titel`, `textdaten/text/Content` (text), `textdaten/fussnoten` and `Footnotes/Footnote` inside the text (footnotes). A `norm` with `gliederungseinheit` is structure; `enbez` "Anlage", "Anhang" or "Muster" is an annex; text "-" or "(weggefallen)" is repealed; these are counted, not kept. `eu_basis` holds the CELEX numbers in the hint "Amtlicher Hinweis des Normgebers auf EG-Recht" up to its closing "+++)".

**Text mapping.** `P`, headings, table rows and preformatted blocks end in a line break. A list (`DL`) stays inside its sentence, as in the official layout, with each item number (`DT`) tied to its text by a no-break space, so "1." never ends a sentence; 280 of the slice's lists continue their sentence after the last item. Editorial comments (`kommentar`), images and footnote marks are left out. Source quirk kept as published: the VerpackDG's hint gives `32025L0040` for Regulation (EU) 2025/40, whose CELEX number would be `32025R0040`.

## 6. Sentence splitting

`js/engine/sentences.js` splits on ". ", "; " and line ends, never after the specification's abbreviations, and adds: no., e.g., i.e., cf. (brief section 5.4); BGBl., ABl. (also spelt "ABI." and "ABL." in the source), Nrn., Halbs., Unterabs., Abschn., Kap., Anh., Anl., lit., Ziff., gem., insb., bzgl., usw., sog., ff., ca., Mio., Mrd., evtl.; single letters before a period ("z. B."); list numbers at a line start or after a colon or semicolon; a day before a month; an ordinal before Tag, Monat, Kalendertag, Lebensjahr and similar nouns; any period before a lowercase word, because no sentence starts with one; and a semicolon inside a citation of the EU's Official Journal ("S. 1; L 335 vom …"). Only an ordinary space, a tab or a line end after the punctuation makes a boundary, which keeps the list numbers of section 5 inside their sentence.

## 7. Fact auditor

`js/engine/auditor.js` implements rules 2 to 6 of the specification with the brief's English additions. Judgement calls: the teens and tens (dreizehn to neunzig, thirteen to ninety) count as number words too, because the listed words alone would let "dreizehn Meldungen" pass; "außer Acht" is not the number eight; option ids are matched before duty ids, because an option id contains one; a § or Art. reference counts as cited when a cited paragraph has that number, and the "Absatz … Satz … Nummer" chain may follow it as it may follow a citation. Violations carry an English message for the model, since tool errors are English in both languages.

Version 1.0.0 closes three gaps the independent review found (REVIEW.md, F2). German number words also count inside compounds: a word made only of number morphemes, with "mal", "fach" or an inflection after it, is a number ("zweihundert", "fünfundzwanzig", "dreitausend", "Hunderttausende", "zweimal", "Dutzende"); "ein", "eins" and "und" join a compound but make none on their own, so "einmal" and "einfach" pass, and an inflection counts only after the words that take one as numbers, so "achten" (to heed) and "elfen" pass; frequency words ("vierteljährlich", "zweijährlich") stay words. English adds "twice", "thrice", "dozen" and the "-fold" forms. An id must have its exact shape (an option id is an option prefix, a cluster or duty id and the option type) and, for decisions and, when the run's options are given, for options, name something the run holds; "O-1500000" and "DEC-2026" are numbers. The search query that `law_search` echoes into the console is shown only when it passes the auditor (both lists of number words); otherwise the console says the query held a number and shows the match count alone.

## 8. Patterns and the gold set

**Matcher.** Case-insensitive with the `u` flag and the boundaries `(?<![\p{L}\p{N}])`, `(?![\p{L}\p{N}])`. A sentence is a candidate with a trigger and a marker (hat, haben, ist, sind, muss, müssen, verpflichtet, obliegt). Extensions, each recorded in `data/patterns/duty-patterns.json` with its source: self-marking phrases ("besteht Auskunftspflicht"), and the present tense of an authority for reporting, informing and publishing (übermittelt, teilt … mit, unterrichtet, veröffentlicht, informiert, erteilt … Bestätigung) as the specification allows it for evaluation and control, because legal German states an authority's duty in the indicative. Discontinuous patterns allow 160 characters between their parts. 57 patterns: the 33 starters of the specification, the authority form of the control verbs that the specification allows (PK-05), and 23 extensions, 21 from the gold set and 2 from the slice. On the bundled snapshot the pattern stage finds 870 candidates in 4,904 sentences.

**Gold set.** Sentences were ordered within each family by SHA-256 of "2026|paragraph id|sentence text", the families interleaved, and every sentence labelled in that order until 40 duties and 20 non-duties were found (262 sentences read). A sentence is a duty when it obliges its addressee to report or notify, prove, inform, document or keep, evaluate or check; an authority's duty in the present tense counts; substantive duties (pay, hand over, design, insure), permissions, definitions, survey scopes and form rules do not. The order is stable under splitter changes elsewhere, so a later rebuild keeps the sample. Victor confirmed the labels on 19 November 2026.

## 9. Cost engine and option rules

Full precision everywhere; only `js/engine/format.js` rounds, with `Intl.NumberFormat` (de-DE, en-GB), for display. A citizen duty's € figure is its material cost; its time is reported in hours, and its options rank by the change in hours first. A register retrieval's administration cost lands on the level in `admin_level`; for a cluster the option sums the deltas of the members the register covers fully and counts the one-off cost once. Options carry `removed_duty_ids` (the duties an approval removes), from which slice coverage counts per type, and fact specifications (key, unit, label code, inputs) that the tools of version 0.2.0 register. A register retrieval carries the risk that the register must hold the data as up to date as needed, as in the specification's Option example.

## 11. Live loop, sub-calls and Sovereign mode

`js/agent.js` adapts Rebate Hunter's loop. Every assistant turn goes back unchanged, thinking blocks and signatures included; tool results come first in the next user message, one per `tool_use` id, and independent `law_*` lookups run in parallel. Requests carry `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01`, the browser header, the 16 strict tools, `tool_choice: auto`, adaptive thinking with summaries, the orchestrator's effort (medium) and no `temperature`, `top_p` or `top_k` (AC-11, checked against `tests/lib/fake-claude.mjs`). Stop rules: a tool call cut off by `max_tokens` is discarded and retried once with half as much room again; another `max_tokens` stop continues; an `end_turn` before `ui_report` gets at most two visible nudges; `refusal` and a full context window end the run. 401 removes the key from the tab, 400 stops with the API's message, transient errors are retried twice (`withRetry`), and every error is audited.

**Caps.** One gate serves the orchestrator and both sub-calls (`createCallGate`): before every call it refuses a 41st call and any call once the run has spent €3; after a call it adds the call's cost (`usageCostUsd` of the call's own model × 0.894, or the sovereign endpoint's euro prices). From 36 calls or €2.70 the next tool result carries one line, "Budget nearly used: request open decisions, then call ui_report." At a cap the run stops with `cap_reached` in the events and the audit, and the closing report comes from the template (`js/report.js`), because no further model call may start.

**Sub-calls** (`js/subcalls.js`, `js/prompt.js`): classification through Haiku 5.5 at effort low with thinking off and the strict `submit_classification` tool, which has no numeric field (AC-37); the amendment through the drafting model with `submit_amendment` (`wording`, and `gist_en` in an English run). Both return the tool call's input; validation, the mapping tables, the one retry and the fact audit stay in the tools.

**Sovereign** (`js/llm/openai-compat.js`): tools go out as functions, `tool_choice: "auto"`, `max_tokens`; `tool_calls` arguments are parsed (arguments that are not JSON come back to the model as an error it can act on), and results return as `tool` messages with the matching `tool_call_id` (AC-12, `tests/lib/fake-openai.mjs`). The assistant message is passed back exactly as the endpoint sent it. With no endpoint configured (D3) the mode stays disabled with its reason.

**The Sovereign message format, as confirmed.** Request: `POST {base_url}/v1/chat/completions` with `model`, `messages`, `tools` as `{ "type": "function", "function": { "name", "description", "parameters" } }`, `tool_choice: "auto"` and `max_tokens`; response: `choices[0].message.tool_calls[]`, each with `id` and `function.arguments` as a JSON string; results back as `{ "role": "tool", "tool_call_id", "content" }` messages after the assistant message. This is the OpenAI chat-completions format that vLLM serves with `--enable-auto-tool-choice` and a `--tool-call-parser` matching the model (vLLM does not support `tool_choice: "required"`, which the adapter never sends). It is confirmed against that documented format and against the scripted stand-in (`tests/lib/fake-openai.mjs`, AC-12), not against a running server, because version 1 has no endpoint (D3). When one is configured, one run of R3's norm in Sovereign mode confirms the format on that server (AC-31, manual), and its result belongs here.

## 12. Tools, run state and replay

The 16 tools live in `js/tools/`, one module each, with their strict schemas in `js/tools/schemas.js` (16 strict tools, 10 optional and no union-typed parameters for the orchestrator, as measured in PLAN.md P7). `js/tools/validate.js` checks every input in code first; a failure, an unknown id or a wrong state returns `is_error` with an English instruction (AC-10). Tools work on the run state (`js/state.js`), which holds candidates, cards, clusters, parameters, facts, options, decisions and the dossier and enforces the review transitions; engines, tools and the run state never touch the DOM, storage or the network (`tools/check-static.mjs`).

**Runs.** R1 to R3 are hand-authored (D7) by `tools/author-runs.mjs`: the tool sequence of a Live run, narration in both languages, the classifications from `tools/runs/rules.mjs` with hand-written cards for the duties the storylines turn on (`tools/runs/overrides.json`), and the drafted wording, among it one deliberately blocked draft each in R1 and R2. Fact placeholders are written by key and resolved by a dry replay. `js/simulation.js` replays a run with the production tools and binds the recorded fact ids first (AC-36); option decisions are always the presenter's, a cluster review accepts only the recorded action, and the closing report comes from the template. `tools/check-runs.mjs` runs `js/run_check.js` and replays every run in both languages (AC-16).

Narration that announces a later decision request names that request's target (`for_request` on the recorded `ui_progress` call): once the presenter's earlier decision has decided the target, the replay skips the request and its announcement together, so nothing narrates a step that never comes (R2's "I put keeping … to a decision" after an approved merge; REVIEW.md, F4). The checker refuses such narration after a decision request without `for_request`, or with a target other than the next request's.

**An approval that an assumption edit withdraws** (REVIEW.md, F1). An approval rests on the figures it was given. When an edit in Assumptions & diagnostics makes an approved option ineligible, the approval lapses: the dossier keeps the item, marked "No longer counted", and stops counting it, so the tiles, the dossier's totals, the exports and the closing report follow the edit; every target the approval had decided is open again; `parameter_changed` names the lapsed options, Diagnostics and the console say so, and a closing report already written is written again from the template, by the app and so without the AI label, with the earlier one marked superseded. An option that applies again after a further edit comes back as a proposal, never as an approval; approving it again replaces the lapsed item. An edit re-drafts only targets whose options were drafted, so a cluster decision's members gain no options of their own (`tests/runs.mjs facts`, `tests/accept.mjs assumptions`).

## 13. Screens, exports and recording

`js/run_controller.js` runs one run at a time: it loads the norms a run needs through the data store (`js/data.js`), creates the run state and the audit log, and drives the replay or the Live loop. Every screen reads the run state; `js/ui.js` re-renders the tiles, the centrepiece and the drawer when the state changes (at most every 30 ms, never under a field the presenter is typing in or choosing from) and the console appends the RunEvents as they arrive. A run keeps the language it started in for its model text (narration, report, draft gist), tagged EN or DE; the code's own sentences (tool lines, labels, figures) follow the switch (AC-38).

- **Control bar** (`js/controls.js`): Start agent opens the mode dialog (`js/dialogs.js`); the run, the slice, the Live model; ×1, ×2, ×4 and Pause for a replay, or in Live and Sovereign mode the cost meter in their place ("N of 40 calls · €x.xx of €3", P-4); Record run with `?record=1`; Abort, Reset, Assumptions & diagnostics. `?speed=n` (up to 1000) replays faster than ×4, for rehearsals and the browser suites.
- **Key dialog:** the key is checked with the free `GET /v1/models` before a Live run, held in this tab's session storage, shown masked and attached only to requests to api.anthropic.com. A 401 during a run removes it. No export, audit entry, recording or console line holds it (AC-23, `tests/accept.mjs key`). An endpoint for Sovereign mode that needs a key gets its own slot, `dr.sovereign.key` (`js/sovereign_key.js`, because `js/keystore.js` is shared with the sister apps unchanged): the Anthropic key never travels to the endpoint, the endpoint's key never to Anthropic, and a 401 from the endpoint forgets only its key (`tests/live.mjs sovereign`).
- **Views:** the duty map (`js/dutymap.js`) counts the duty cards per norm and type on an ink ramp of seven steps (the surface for none, then six of Vici's ink mixed into the surface), the printed count light on the two darkest (contrast checked per step in both themes by `tests/layout.mjs`); a cell opens its duty cards (`js/dutycard.js`). The duplicates board (`js/duplicates.js`) sorts clusters by their best option's relief. Options & review (`js/options_panel.js`) records a decision through `state.decide`, whose refusals (no code, a code that does not fit, a missing or long comment) appear under the buttons in the active language (AC-19).
- **Figures:** every figure the engines compute is a button that opens "Where does this figure come from?" (`js/popovers.js`) with the fact's label, value, producing tool and inputs, each input with its source or the label Assumption. Other numbers on screen are no figures of the run: ids, reason codes, § references, the dates and versions of provenance lines, and the benchmarks −⅓ and −½ in the gauges' labels, which are the specification's targets. The keep option names its known data uses instead of counting them (`tests/accept.mjs figures` scans the tiles, the option cards and the dossier for any other number).
- **Assumptions & diagnostics** (`js/diagnostics.js`): the cost parameters of the duties the run has priced, editable inline (an edit recomputes facts, options and tiles and is logged as `parameter_changed`; a value is read in the active language, "9.000" in German and "9,000" in English as nine thousand, and a separator that would be read the other way is refused, never guessed, by `parseLocalNumber` in `js/engine/format.js`), the law snapshot, the self-test (§15), the policy with the key's state and the sovereign endpoint, checked against the specification's policy for the configured endpoint (`js/policy.js`, which `tools/set-csp.mjs` writes into `index.html` too), the model and caps from `config/run.json` (mirrored in `js/config.js`, checked by `tests/unit.mjs`), and the run's figures with the run checker's verdict on a recording.
- **Exports** (`js/exports.js`, `js/outbox.js`): the dossier as Markdown and as a self-contained HTML page, the audit log as JSON (the log with the run's mode, model, settings, facts and decisions) and the duty list as CSV (byte order mark, semicolons, decimal commas, the same columns in both languages). File names follow the active language: `relief-dossier-<run>-<date-time>.md` or `entlastungsdossier-…`, `duty-radar-audit-…` or `pflichten-radar-audit-…`, `duty-radar-duties-<slice>-<date>.csv` or `pflichten-radar-pflichten-…`. Each download is logged as `export_created`.
- **Recording** (`js/recorder.js`): in Live or Sovereign mode with `?record=1`, Record run downloads the plan, the narration, the tool calls with their inputs, the cluster reviews, the fact ids with their keys and the sub-calls' answers (collected through the loop's `onSubcall` hook), without tool results, decisions, reports, reasoning summaries, thinking blocks or anything key-like. Its texts carry the run's language only; curation adds the other language, each request's intention and the `expect_audit` marks before `tools/check-runs.mjs` accepts it (`source` "recorded" with `recorded_with` the model).

## 14. AI Act: the risk class

The definition of done asks for a check of Duty Radar's risk class with the online tool of the Bundesnetzagentur's KI-Service Desk ("Online-Tool zur ersten Einschätzung der Risikoklasse", linked from <https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Fachthemen/Digitales/KI/start_ki.html>). The tool is an interactive questionnaire on a website that the build environment cannot operate, so Victor runs it and his result is recorded here (README, acceptance record). The builder's reading, which is not legal advice and which the tool's answer overrides:

- **What it is.** A prototype that supports a person who reviews statutory duties: it finds duties in law texts, groups duplicate data requests, prices options with a published method and drafts amendment wording, and a person decides every option. It takes no decision with an effect on anyone, and it is not placed on the market.
- **Not prohibited** (Art. 5): none of the listed practices applies.
- **Probably not high-risk** (Art. 6 with Annex III): the annex's areas are biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, access to essential services and benefits, law enforcement, migration and asylum, and the administration of justice and democratic processes. Support for preparing changes to law is none of them; in particular it neither assists a judicial authority in applying the law to facts nor influences an election or referendum.
- **Transparency** (Art. 50): the header of every screen says "You are interacting with an AI system." / "Sie interagieren mit einem KI-System.", and every text the model writes carries an AI label, the draft wording also "[Entwurf, rechtlich zu prüfen]".
- **Built in regardless of the class:** human oversight (only a reviewer's approval with a reason code reaches the dossier), logging of every model call, tool call, decision and parameter change (`js/audit.js`), no personal data, and the AI-literacy duty of Art. 4 left to whoever deploys it. Obligations for the model itself rest with its provider.
- **Result of the Bundesnetzagentur's tool:** pending with Victor; the answers given and the class it returns belong here, with the date.

## 15. Version 1.0.0: hardening and demo
